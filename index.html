UK company data: explained, connected, usable

UK company data is free, large and genuinely useful — and awkward to use. It is spread across several registers, published in formats built for filing rather than analysis, and shaped by rules that are easy to misread. This site does three things: it explains what Companies House and the other UK sources publish, it connects them by showing how they join and what each adds, and it publishes tested, ready-to-use datasets built from them. Where code adds something beyond what already exists, we add it; where it doesn’t, we link to the best existing explanation instead.

This is methodology and open data, not a company-rating product. Register records can support due-diligence signals; they cannot establish that a company is trustworthy, solvent or non-fraudulent. Nothing here produces a composite score, publishes named company results, or includes personal data.

Three ways in

Guide

Where the data is and how it fits together. What Companies House publishes, how the UK sources join, what the accounts data is, and the pitfalls we hit reading it.

Datasets

Ready-to-use, tested tables. An open 2014–2026 accounts dataset (WIDE and LONG), how we know it is right, and the ownership / PSC work still in progress.

Indicators

Transparent register flags. A documented rule registry over Companies House data, how it is validated, and what it cannot show — per-company flags, never a single score.

Start here

Pick the path that matches what you want.

  • You want data to analyse. Start with the accounts dataset — one row per company per accounting period, 2014–2026, with a quickstart snippet and a column dictionary. Read the pitfalls before modelling; several of them (dashes, restatements, mis-tagged employee counts) change results quietly if missed.
  • You want code. The pipeline is on GitHub. The accounts guide explains the source format, and the validation page explains how the extractor was checked against two independent parsers. The indicator rules are generated from code, so the documentation cannot drift from the implementation.
  • You want evidence. The landscape covers who uses UK company data and what the commercial market charges. Prior work summarises what others have built, and the evidence register is where sources are catalogued as they are reviewed.

What this is, and is not

  • No composite score, anywhere. The indicator pipeline records discrete, registrar-sourced facts as flags with a severity and an evidence string. Severity grades the seriousness of the recorded state, not confidence in it. There is no single number.
  • No named results, no personal data. The repository publishes code, methodology and synthetic fixtures only. Run outputs, officer/PSC-level data and named flag lists stay private. The published accounts datasets contain financial facts and company numbers only.
  • Open licence. Source data is Crown copyright, published under the Open Government Licence v3.0 and free to reuse with attribution. The OGL does not cover personal data, which is part of why personal data is removed before anything is published.

Reading the confidence labels

Figures across this site carry one of three labels, and each carries its source’s confidence — the label is not an independent re-verification by this site unless it says so:

  • [Checked] — either a project measurement traceable to a committed repo file (e.g. dataset coverage, the 99.999% parser agreement, iXBRL adoption), or an official/primary source as recorded in the repo’s source material (e.g. the register statistics from the Companies House annual report). Where it is the latter, the label inherits that source’s confidence; it has not been independently re-derived here. Project measurements are tagged “(project measurement)”.
  • [Estimated] — a reputable secondary source or a study estimate (e.g. the user-value figures, most commercial prices). Treat as indicative.
  • [Not verified] — flagged uncertain by its own source, or not re-checked here (e.g. the FAME and Beauhurst prices, the API rate limit, a civil-service spend total). Confirm before relying on it.

Commercial prices are indicative, dated to the source, and negotiable; legislation dates (the 2025 thresholds, the 2028 reforms) are given with their confirmation date because they have moved before.
