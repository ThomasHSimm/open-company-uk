Accounts dataset (WIDE & LONG, 2014–2026)

Summary

This is an open, analysis-ready version of twelve years of UK company accounts, built from Companies House’s free monthly bulk archives. It comes in two tables. WIDE is one row per company per accounting period, with the core balance-sheet figures as columns — the table most people want for modelling. LONG is a fuller, personal-data-removed table — one row per fact, reaching the concepts the curated columns leave out — for anyone who needs more than WIDE. Coverage is continuous from January 2014 to August 2026 (152 monthly archives, no gaps), and both published tables contain financial facts and company numbers only — no names, addresses or other personal data. The complete as-filed archive (which does contain director names, addresses and loans) is kept private and never published; what you can download is the filtered subset described under Licence below.

The one decision you must make before using WIDE is which pivot to read: as_first_reported (what was known at the time — use this for anything predictive) or latest (includes later restatements — do not use it for prediction). Everything else on this page is about using the tables correctly: the columns, the known limitations, and the licence.

Details

The two tables

Table Grain Contents Size
WIDE one row per (company, period-end) nine core balance-sheet concepts + equity and creditors splits + helper columns ~2.6 GB Parquet
LONG (public subset) one row per fact the full concept set (~4,593 concepts) with person-related and free-text concepts removed — not the raw as-filed archive ~9.2 GB Parquet, one file per year

“LONG” here means the published, personal-data-removed subset. The full LONG archive used internally to build these tables contains personal data — director names, addresses, the signing director, director loans and remuneration — and is never published. Only the filtered subset (numeric person-concept denylist + structured-field allowlist, described under Licence) is downloadable. The Kaggle “LONG” dataset is that subset, not the raw archive.

Coverage: 152 monthly archives, continuous 2014-01 → 2026-08, 0 gaps. WIDE has 33,513,017 rows in as_first_reported mode and 36,710,673 in latest mode. Source: docs/accounts-wide-rebuild-verification.md, docs/accounts-limitations.md. [Checked] (project measurement).

Why it starts in 2014, not 2008. The format WIDE reads (iXBRL) was adopted gradually — ~0% of filings in 2010, ~97% by 2014 — so 2014 is where tagged coverage becomes near-universal, not where Companies House’s data stops. Pre-2014 would be a separate, much lower-fill-rate extraction. Coverage gaps within the span are non-random — they correlate with filing regime, exemption status and company size, so do not treat non-appearance as a random exclusion. Source: docs/accounts-limitations.md.

Most rows are sparse, and that is expected. A company reporting only Equity and CurrentAssets for a year genuinely filed filleted accounts without the rest — it is not a gap in the extraction. See the accounts guide.

as_first_reported vs latest

Both files share the same schema; they differ only in which filing wins each cell.

  • accounts-wide-as_first_reported.parquet (flagship, default). Each cell holds the value from the earliest filing that reported it — only information available at the time, no look-ahead. Use this for any predictive or point-in-time work.
  • accounts-wide-latest.parquet (secondary). Each cell holds the value from the most recent filing, which can include later comparative restatements of an earlier period. Do not use this for predictive work — a value can reflect information that postdates the period.

Across the full span, 9.36% of repeated (company, period, concept) values disagree between first-reported and a later filing, which is exactly why the two files are kept separate rather than collapsed into one ambiguous “current” column. Source: docs/accounts-validation-summary.md, docs/accounts-restatement-2014-2026.md.

Quickstart

The files are Parquet, so polars, pandas, DuckDB or Spark all work. row_available_yyyymm is the month by which every value in a row had been filed, which makes point-in-time filtering a single comparison. The company number below (00012345) is a placeholder — substitute a real one.

import polars as pl

wide = pl.read_parquet("accounts-wide-as_first_reported.parquet")

# one company's history (company numbers are TEXT — keep the leading zeros)
(wide
   .filter(pl.col("company") == "00012345")
   .sort("period_end"))

# only what was known as of a date: no look-ahead
wide.filter(pl.col("row_available_yyyymm") <= 202312)

Rules of thumb, each because it changes how the data should be used:

  1. Pivot mode — as_first_reported for prediction; latest leaks the future (~9% of repeated figures differ).
  2. Creditors — use creditors_within_one_year / creditors_after_one_year; the bare total is ~4% populated and unreliable even when present.
  3. Employees — ~29% are GBP-tagged (median value 1.0, mostly real headcounts). The flag marks them; no value is removed. Don’t filter employees by unit.
  4. Dashes — a dash in the accounts means nil and is stored as 0, not null.
  5. Missing is not zero — values are never imputed, and some missingness is non-random, so decide explicitly how to handle gaps.

Source: deck slides 15–16 and notes (docs/source-material/companies-house-accounts-deck.md); see also pitfalls.

Limitations to know before modelling

  • Balance-sheet only for most companies (filleted filing): no turnover or profit.
  • Starts in 2014 — earlier bulk data is barely machine-tagged.
  • Some breakdowns skipped — facts in multi-member or custom (typed-member) dimensional contexts are not captured; ~38% of filings contain at least one such context (these are mostly detailed notes, not the balance-sheet totals WIDE uses).
  • Nine core concepts validated — the other ~4,580 concepts in LONG are captured as filed but not validated; check the source filing before relying on a rare one.
  • Restatements (~9%) and an employee reporting break around 2020–2021.
  • Non-random gaps — e.g. equity fill-rate varies by more than 10 percentage points across months.
  • GBP only in WIDE — non-sterling filers are excluded (kept in LONG), not converted.

Full detail and every reconciliation: docs/accounts-limitations.md. Source: deck slide 17.

Licence and personal-data policy

Contains public sector information from Companies House, licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0, free to reuse with attribution. Companies House and the UK Government do not endorse this dataset, and a derived dataset is not a complete or official representation of any company.

Both published tables are financial facts and company numbers only. WIDE carries no names, addresses or director figures. LONG applies a maintainer-approved filter: a 95-concept numeric denylist (director/officer/key-management/related-party remuneration, loans and advances) and a 17-concept non-numeric allowlist of structured fields only (dates, the registered number, filing-software name, and categorical flags) — every other non-numeric concept, including all free text, is dropped. The private, unfiltered archive that includes personal data is never published. The OGL does not cover personal data, which is part of why this split exists. Source: docs/accounts-public-long-concepts.md, docs/accounts-limitations.md.
